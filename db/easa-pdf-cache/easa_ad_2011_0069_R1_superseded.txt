E Landing Gear – Main Landing Gear (MLG) Door Actuator –

ATA 32 Monitoring / Inspection S

Manufacturer(s): Airbus (formerly Airbus Industrie) R

Applicability: Airbus A318-111, A318-112, A318-121, A318-122, A319-111, A319-112, A319-113, A319-114, A319-115, A319-131, A319-132, A319-133, A320-111, A320-211, A320-212, A320-214, A320-215, A320-216, A320-231, A320-232, E A320-233, A321-111, A321-112, A321-131, A321-211, A321-212, A321-213, A321-231 and A321-232 aeroplanes, all manufacturer serial numbers, if equipped with MLG door actuators having Part Number (P/N) 114122006, P/N 114122007, P/N 114122009, P/N 114122010, P/N 114122011 or P/N P 114122012.

Reason: Some operators reported slow operation of the MLG door opening/closing sequence, leading to the generation of ECAM warnings during the landing gear retraction or extension sequence. U Investigations showed that the damping ring and associated retaining ring of the MLG door actuator deteriorate. The resultant debris increases the friction inside the actuator which can be sufficiently high to restrict opening of the S MLG door by gravity, during operation of the landing gear alternate (free-fall) extension system. This condition, if not corrected, could prevent the full extension and/or downlocking of the MLG, possibly resulting in MLG collapse during landing or rollout and consequent damage to the aeroplane and injury to occupants. AD No.: 2011-0069R1 and to introduce as an optional terminating action Airbus production Modification 38274 and associated Service Bulletin (SB) A320-32-1338, which incorporate an improved retaining ring, located on the piston rod's extension end, and a new piston rod with machined shoulder to accommodate the thicker section of the modified retaining ring. After in-service introduction of the new MLG door actuator, P/N 114122012, several operators reported failures of internal parts of the MLG door actuator. Investigations confirmed that these failures could result in slow extension of the actuator rod, delaying the MLG Door operation, or possibly stopping just before the end of the stroke, preventing the door to reach the fully open position. To address this potential unsafe condition, EASA issued AD 2011-0069, which superseded EASA AD 2006-0112R1, to require an amendment of the applicable Airplane Flight Manual (AFM), repetitive checks of specific Centralized Fault Display System (CFDS) messages, repetitive inspections of the opening sequence of the MLG door actuator and, depending on findings, D corrective action(s). This AD is revised to recognise that accomplishment of repetitive inspections and corrective actions in accordance with the instructions of Airbus SB A320- E 32-1390 Revision 01 is an acceptable alternative method to comply with certain actions required by this AD.
