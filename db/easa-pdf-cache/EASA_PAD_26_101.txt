EASA CRD of PAD No. 26-101 COMMENT RESPONSE DOCUMENT EASA PAD No.: 26-101 Published on 07 September 2026 and officially closed for comments on 05 October 2026 Commenter 1: TAP Air Portugal – Margarida Almeida Azevedo – 08/09/2026 Comment # 1 We were aware that this AD would come up but we were expecting to be able to accomplish it in either our 6-year C-check or the 12-year C-check. This is important because the modification is very time-consuming and requires opening a lot of access panels that are not normally opened during a regular C-check. We already performed the 6-year C-check for every affected MSN we have, and the last 12 year C-check will only take place in 2032. Since the AD indicates that the modification should be applied in 61 months, the limit date to apply the modification will be around September/October 2031. We would like to know if there is any chance that EASA would consider a larger time limit to apply the modification so that we can apply it on our 12 year C-checks (which will end in the beginning of summer of 2032)? Thank you in advance for your help and understanding. EASA response: Comment not agreed. The defined compliance time has already considered margins to allow all produced aircraft reaching their first C check to restore the compliance of the design. EASA reminds that EU operators, in case of urgent operational needs, can request from their National Aviation Authority (State of Registry) a temporary exemption under the provisions of Article 71 of Regulation (EU) 2018/1139, or an AMOC process (proposing compensative measures) could also be engaged. No changes have been made to the Final AD in response to this comment. Page 1 of 2 TE.CAP.00115-007 © European Union Aviation Safety Agency. All rights reserved. ISO9001 Certified. EASA CRD of PAD No. 26-101 Commenter 2: Delta Air Lines Inc. – Alanah Buinauskas – 24/09/2026 Comment # 2 Can you please clarify what the below “Reserved” section of PAD 26-101 is for? Will there be a new modification requirement upon publication of the AD? EASA response: Comment noted. This is a standard practice, established for a while. Paragraph (2) is an editorial ‘trick’ to allocate a paragraph number to the single/first requirements of the AD. In case of revision of the AD (e.g., adding a paragraph (2) listing acceptable methods of compliance), the above will allow keeping the initial requirement listed as paragraph (1), which is expected to ease tracking AD requirements to actions already accomplished on a specific aeroplane.
