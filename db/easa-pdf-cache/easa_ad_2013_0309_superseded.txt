Applicability: Airbus A318-111, A318-112, A318-121, A318-122, A319-111, A319-112, A319-113, A319-114, A319-115, A319-131, A319-132, A319-133, A320-211, A320-212, A320-214, A320-215, A320-216, A320-231, A320-232, A320-233, E A321-111, A321-112, A321-131, A321-211, A321-212, A321-213, A321-231 and A321-232 aeroplanes, all manufacturer serial numbers.

Reason: One case of elevator servo-control disconnection was reported on an aeroplane P of the A320 family. Investigation results revealed that the failure occurred at the servo-control rod eye-end. Prompted by this finding, additional inspections revealed cracking at the same location on a number of other servo-control rod eye-ends. In several cases, both actuators of the same elevator surface were U affected. It has been determined that the detected rod end cracks are caused by fatigue, induced by a bending effect which is linked to the spherical bearing rotational torque. As the elevator surface is neither actuated nor damped, a dual servo- S control disconnection on the same elevator would result in an uncontrolled surface. This condition, if not corrected, could result in reduced control of the aeroplane. AD No.: 2013-0309 since aeroplane first flight and, in case of findings, accomplishment of corrective actions. As a result of this AD, a significant number of rod eye-ends have been found cracked. In addition, some cracks have been reported on rod eye-ends that had not yet accumulated the 10 000 FC of the established threshold. Prompted by these findings, EASA issued AD 2010-0046, which partially retained the initial inspection requirement of EASA AD 2008-0149, which was superseded, reduced the compliance time of the initial inspection and introduced a repetitive inspection programme. Subsequently, EASA AD 2010-0046 was revised to specify that repetitive inspections and corrective actions of an elevator servo-control rod-end at part level was an acceptable alternative method to comply with the actions required by this AD. In addition, some editorial changes were made (Table 1) for reasons of standardisation. Since EASA AD 2010-0046R1 was issued, a new elevator servo-control rod D eye-end has been developed, incorporating a re-greasable roller bearing. For the reason described above, this AD retains the requirements of EASA AD 2010-0046R1, which is superseded, and introduces an optional terminating E action for the repetitive inspections by replacement of the existing elevator servo-control rod eye-ends with the new elevator servo-control rod eye-end. In addition, this AD prohibits, for aeroplanes that incorporate this optional modification, (re)installation of unmodified elevator servo-controls. D This AD also deletes the Model A320-111 from the Applicability, as its approval has been withdrawn and it is no longer listed in the TCDS. This AD is republished to correct and clarify paragraph (8). E
