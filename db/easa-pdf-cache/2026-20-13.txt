ACTION: Final rule. SUMMARY: The FAA is adopting a new airworthiness directive (AD) for certain The Boeing Company Model 737- 600, -700, -700C, -800, -900, and -900ER series airplanes. This AD was prompted by a report that some passenger service unit (PSU)-mounted video monitors became detached from the PSU rails during a hard landing. This AD requires replacing PSU-mounted video monitors that do not have secondary retention lanyards. The FAA is issuing this AD to address the unsafe condition on these products. SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION: Background The FAA issued a notice of proposed rulemaking (NPRM) to amend 14 CFR part 39 by adding an AD that would apply to certain The Boeing Company Model 737-600, -700, -700C, -800, -900, and -900ER series airplanes. The NPRM was published in the Federal Register on January 17, 2025 (90 FR 5751). The NPRM was prompted by a report that some PSU-mounted video monitors became detached from the PSU rails during a hard landing that did not exceed the emergency load requirements for the PSUs. In the NPRM, the FAA proposed to require replacing PSU-mounted video monitors that do not have secondary retention lanyards. The FAA is issuing this AD to address PSU- mounted video monitors that could become fully detached or be held at an unsafe height, which, if not addressed, could result in injury to passengers and impede passenger and crew egress during emergency evacuation. Discussion of Final Airworthiness Directive Comments The FAA received a comment from Boeing, who supported the NPRM without change. The FAA received additional comments from five commenters, including All Nippon Airways Co., Ltd. (ANA), Aviation Partners Boeing, Delta Air Lines, Inc. (Delta), Panasonic Avionics Corporation (Panasonic), and an individual. The following presents the comments received on the NPRM and the FAA's response to each comment. Effect of Winglets on Accomplishment of the Proposed Actions Aviation Partners Boeing stated that the installation of blended or split scimitar winglets per supplemental type certificate (STC) ST00830SE does not affect compliance with the mandated actions in the proposed rule. The FAA agrees with the commenter that STC ST00830SE does not affect compliance with this AD. Therefore, the installation of STC ST00830SE does not affect the ability to accomplish the actions required by this AD. The FAA did not change this AD in response to this comment. Request To Revise Paragraph (e) To Clarify Safety Concerns Panasonic requested that the FAA revise paragraph (e) of the proposed AD to include the statement, “Although some models of PSU-mounted video monitors have not become detached under such landings, we have determined that this AD is warranted due to relative motion between the PSU rails that drive forces exceeding the approved emergency landing conditions for the attached PSU-mounted monitors.” Panasonic asserted that the improvement action for Panasonic monitors involves an enhancement to its fail-safe design and is not a result of correcting any defect or noncompliance in the PSU-mounted monitor assemblies themselves. Panasonic requested this change to avoid potentially misleading operators that Panasonic monitors have become detached because they did not meet the existing emergency landing requirements they were designed to meet. The FAA acknowledges the commenter's concern. The FAA is unaware of Panasonic monitors that have become detached during hard landings that did not exceed emergency landing requirements. However, paragraph (e) of this AD specifies that some, not all, PSU-mounted video monitors became detached. The FAA did not change this AD in response to this comment. Request To Include Panasonic Service Bulletin SB141908-44-001 as an Accepted Method of Compliance Panasonic requested that the FAA include Panasonic Service Bulletin SB141908-44-001 as an accepted method of compliance for the proposed AD but did not provide the specified service bulletin to the FAA. Panasonic asserted that the effectivity of Boeing Special Attention Requirements Bulletin 737-25- 1858 RB does not include airplanes with STCs that have identical parts that would be affected by the NPRM and suggested that adding this service bulletin as an accepted method of compliance would reduce the FAA's efforts to support alternative methods of compliance (AMOC) requests to address airplanes installed with FAA STC ST02908NY. The FAA disagrees. Paragraph (g)(3) of this AD requires airplanes not identified in the effectivity paragraph of either Boeing service bulletin to perform the required actions using an AMOC. Authority for This Rulemaking Title 49 of the United States Code specifies the FAA's authority to issue rules on aviation safety. Subtitle I, section 106, describes the authority of the FAA Administrator. Subtitle VII: Aviation Programs, describes in more detail the scope of the Agency's authority. The FAA is issuing this rulemaking under the authority described in Subtitle VII, Part A, Subpart III, Section 44701: General requirements. Under that section, Congress charges the FAA with promoting safe flight of civil aircraft in air commerce by prescribing regulations for practices, methods, and procedures the Administrator finds necessary for safety in air commerce. This regulation is within the scope of that authority because it addresses an unsafe condition that is likely to exist or develop on products identified in this rulemaking action.
