ATA 34 Navigation – Angle of Attack (AoA) Sensors – Replacement S

Manufacturer(s): Airbus (formerly Airbus Industrie)

Applicability: Airbus A318-111, A318-112, A318-121, A318-122, A319-111, A319-112, R A319-113, A319-114, A319-115, A319-131, A319-132, A319-133, A320-111, A320-211, A320-212, A320-214, A320-215, A320-216, A320-231, A320-232, A320-233, A321-111, A321-112, A321-131, A321-211, A321-212, A321-213, A321-231 and A321-232 aeroplanes, all manufacturer serial numbers. E

Reason: During Airbus Final Assembly Line flight tests, AoA data from two different aeroplanes was found inaccurate, which was confirmed by flight data analysis. P The results of the investigation conducted by Airbus and Thales on the removed sensors revealed oil residue between the stator and the rotor parts of the AoA vane position resolvers. This oil residue was the result of incorrect removal of machining oil during the manufacturing process of the AoA U resolvers. At low temperatures, this oil residue becomes viscous (typically in cruise), causing delayed and/or reduced AoA vane movement. Multiple AoA sensors could be simultaneously affected, providing incorrect indications of the AoA of the aeroplane. S This condition, if not corrected, could lead to erroneous AoA information and consequent delayed activation or non-activation of the AoA protection systems which, if during flight at a high angle of attack, could result in reduced control of the aeroplane. AD No.: 2012-0236R1 (formerly SEXTANT) Part Number (P/N) C16291AA AoA sensor and the replacement of all suspect units with serviceable ones. EASA AD 2011-0203 also prohibited the (re)installation of these same s/n sensors on any aeroplane, unless corrective measures had been accomplished. Since that AD was issued, it was discovered that a part of the affected population of AoA sensors may have been modified and re-identified from P/N C16291AA to P/N C16291AB, in accordance with the instructions of Airbus Service Bulletin (SB) A320-34-1444, without having passed the inspection in accordance with the instructions of Thales Avionics SB C16291A-34-007 Revision 01. In addition, to anticipate compliance with new airworthiness regulations concerning icing, new conic plates had been developed to improve the protection of AoA sensors from ice crystals. Consequently, EASA issued AD 2012-0236, retaining the requirements of EASA AD 2011-0203, which was superseded, and required, for the affected D population that was not addressed by EASA AD 2011-0203, the replacement of the suspect units with serviceable ones, and modification of all AoA sensor plates, which consist of replacement of the current AoA flat plates with the new conic plates for all Thales Avionics (formerly SEXTANT) Part Number E (P/N) C16291AA, P/N C16291AB and Goodrich P/N 0861ED AoA sensors. Since that AD was issued, an Airbus A330 aeroplane, equipped with AoA sensors having conic plates, experienced a blockage of all AoA sensors during climb, leading to autopilot disconnection and activation of the alpha D protection when the Mach number was increased. Based on the results of the analysis of this occurrence, it is suspected that the conic plates may have contributed to the event. The design of the affected AoA conic plate on A330/A340 aeroplane is similar to that installed on A320 family aeroplanes. E Investigations are on-going to determine what caused the blockage of AoA sensors. For the reasons described above, EASA AD 2012-0236 is revised to remove S the requirement to install the AoA conic plate, deleting paragraphs (4), (6) and (7) from the AD. At the same time, prompted by operator comments and queries, paragraph (5) has been revised for clarification. This revised AD is considered an interim and preventive measure. R
